<section id="what-is-gpa">
  <h2>What is GPA?</h2>
  <p>
    Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis is a rare autoimmune disease causing inflammation in small- to medium-sized blood vessels, most commonly affecting the sinuses, lungs, and kidneys. While serious, early diagnosis and targeted therapies allow many people to achieve long-term remission.
  </p>
</section>

<section id="research-guidelines">
  <h2>Research, Guidelines &amp; Treatments</h2>

  <h3>Guidelines</h3>
  <ul>
    <li><a href="https://ard.bmj.com/content/83/1/30" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>EULAR Guidelines (Medical):</strong></a> Hellmich et al., European recommendations for managing ANCA-associated vasculitis (2022 update), Annals of the Rheumatic Diseases, 2024. Recommends rituximab or cyclophosphamide plus steroids for organ-threatening disease, tapering steroids to 5 mg prednisolone equivalent per day by 4 to 5 months, and rituximab for maintenance. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1136/ard-2022-223764" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">DOI</a> | <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36927642/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">PubMed</a></li>
    <li><a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keaf240" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>British Society for Rheumatology Recommendations (2025):</strong></a> Biddle et al., 26 recommendations covering GPA and MPA treatment, service standards, and patient education, including a dedicated section on subglottic stenosis and ENT care. Rheumatology, 2025. <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40499922/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">PubMed</a> | <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12316377/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">PMC</a></li>
    <li><a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.kint.2023.10.008" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>KDIGO 2024 Guideline for ANCA-Associated Vasculitis:</strong></a> The kidney-focused international guideline. Recommends rituximab or cyclophosphamide plus steroids for new disease, faster steroid tapering, and avacopan as a steroid-sparing option. Kidney International, 2024. <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38388102/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">PubMed</a></li>
    <li><a href="https://vasculitisfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/GPA-MPA-Relatred-Conditions.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>ACR / Vasculitis Foundation Patient Document (reviewed February 2024):</strong></a> Recommendations for conditions related to active GPA/MPA, based on the 2021 ACR/Vasculitis Foundation guideline. Specifically covers plasma exchange for related conditions such as kidney inflammation and lung bleeding.</li>
  </ul>

  <h3>Rituximab maintenance, relapse, and remission</h3>
  <ul>
    <li><a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11393786/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Rituximab Research Overview:</strong></a> Papuashvili et al., systematic review, Cureus, 2024. Across 11 included studies, rituximab improved remission rates and reduced relapse frequency compared with cyclophosphamide and azathioprine, including in relapsing disease. <a href="https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.66838" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">DOI</a> | <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39280383/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">PubMed</a></li>
    <li><a href="https://doi.org/10.22088/cjim.16.3.480" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Rituximab versus azathioprine for maintenance in GPA:</strong></a> Alesaeidi et al., Caspian Journal of Internal Medicine, 2025. In 64 GPA patients, rituximab maintenance had far fewer relapses than azathioprine (18.6 percent versus 66.6 percent over follow-up) with similar side effects. <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40786576/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">PubMed</a> | <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12329910/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">PMC</a></li>
    <li><a href="https://doi.org/10.1136/rmdopen-2025-005504" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Spacing rituximab infusions by individual relapse risk:</strong></a> Bartoletti et al., RMD Open, 2025. After standard 18-month maintenance, spacing infusions to every 12 months for intermediate-risk patients kept disease controlled over 24 months, suggesting infusion timing can be tailored to relapse risk instead of one fixed schedule. <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40480651/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">PubMed</a> | <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12161294/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">PMC</a></li>
    <li><a href="https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm14010208" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>How we treat ANCA-associated vasculitis: maintenance therapy:</strong></a> Roccatello et al., Journal of Clinical Medicine, 2025. Review concluding rituximab has been the preferred maintenance treatment since 2021, with retreatment decisions guided by each patient's flare risk. <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39797292/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">PubMed</a> | <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11721047/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">PMC</a></li>
  </ul>

  <h3>Reducing steroids: avacopan</h3>
  <ul>
    <li><a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keaf122" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Avacopan in patients aged 65 and older (ADVOCATE trial analysis):</strong></a> Geetha et al., Rheumatology, 2025. In older patients, avacopan worked as well as a prednisone taper for remission and sustained remission, with much lower steroid doses and less steroid toxicity. <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40037556/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">PubMed</a> | <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12107037/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">PMC</a></li>
    <li><a href="https://doi.org/10.1136/rmdopen-2025-005743" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Avacopan with cyclophosphamide (ADVOCATE subgroup):</strong></a> Geetha et al., RMD Open, 2025. Avacopan gave similar remission rates to a prednisone taper, with fewer relapses, faster kidney improvement, and lower steroid toxicity, showing avacopan works alongside cyclophosphamide, not only with rituximab. <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41052893/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">PubMed</a> | <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12506455/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">PMC</a></li>
  </ul>

  <h3>Real-world evidence</h3>
  <ul>
    <li><a href="https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm14217884" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>GPA in a Polish tertiary centre, 2010-2025:</strong></a> Hus et al., Journal of Clinical Medicine, 2025. In 50 GPA patients, ear-nose-throat disease was very common (rhinosinusitis 76.0 percent, subglottic stenosis 34.0 percent), and practice shifted over time toward rituximab-based treatment, steroid minimisation, and early avacopan use. <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41227282/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">PubMed</a> | <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12607949/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">PMC</a></li>
    <li><a href="https://clinicaltrials.gov" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>ClinicalTrials.gov:</strong></a> Search <em>"granulomatosis with polyangiitis"</em> to monitor active clinical studies and CAR-T trials.</li>
  </ul>

<section id="stenosis">
  <h2>GPA &amp; Subglottic Stenosis</h2>
  <p>Subglottic stenosis, narrowing of the airway just below the vocal cords, is a known GPA complication and often needs both airway procedures and systemic treatment. These recent studies focus on it directly.</p>
  <ul>
    <li><a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keag107" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Clinical outcomes of subglottic stenosis in GPA (STOP-GPA, international multicentre study):</strong></a> Gallina et al., Rheumatology, 2026. In 89 GPA patients with subglottic stenosis, 54 percent had two or more flares; systemic immunosuppression meant fewer relapses than local treatment alone, cyclophosphamide induction gave the longest relapse-free survival, and steroid-only treatment meant more relapses. <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41734284/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">PubMed</a></li>
    <li><a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/lary.70249" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Immunosuppression and surgery-free interval in GPA airway stenosis:</strong></a> Torres et al., The Laryngoscope, 2026. In 55 patients followed a median 10.2 years, time without needing another airway procedure averaged 45 months during therapeutic rituximab exposure or remission, versus 20 months otherwise. <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41178694/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">PubMed</a> | <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12993087/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">PMC</a></li>
    <li><a href="https://doi.org/10.1055/a-2357-8895" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Balloon dilatation of subglottic stenoses, especially in GPA:</strong></a> Wichmann et al., Laryngorhinootologie, 2025 (article text in German; abstract available in English). In 14 patients, balloon dilation improved breathing and health scores for all patients after the first procedure; narrower stenoses under 4 mm needed only one procedure, while 12 mm balloons meant repeated treatments. <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38996431/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">PubMed</a></li>
    <li><a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/lary.70167" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>C-reactive protein values in idiopathic and autoimmune subglottic stenosis:</strong></a> Neevel et al., The Laryngoscope, 2026. In 59 idiopathic and 47 GPA-related stenosis patients, CRP was mildly elevated in many patients and high CRP was more frequent in GPA-related stenosis, but CRP at presentation did not predict recurrence, so it is not a useful warning marker for relapse. <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40990336/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">PubMed</a> | <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12913726/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">PMC</a></li>
    <li><a href="https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm14051532" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>A 28-year-old man with stridor and dyspnea (case report, single patient):</strong></a> Bertuccio et al., Journal of Clinical Medicine, 2025. A single patient with GPA confined to tracheobronchial stenosis was treated with rigid bronchoscopy plus systemic immunosuppression and stayed in remission at two years. This illustrates combined airway plus systemic treatment but proves nothing on its own. <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40095461/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">PubMed</a> | <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11900471/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">PMC</a></li>
  </ul>
